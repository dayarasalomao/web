## The distinction this page exists to make

**Screening and investigation are different things, and conflating them is the
most likely error on this page.**

- **Screening** applies to people **without symptoms**. USPSTF: "asymptomatic adults 45 years or older who are at average risk". `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)
- **A change in bowel habit is a symptom.** It moves the person out of the screening pathway and into an investigation pathway, where age thresholds are lower and the test is chosen by suspicion. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #2)

A patient who reads "screening starts at 45" and concludes their symptom at 38
can wait has read the page wrong. Say it explicitly.

## 1. Definition

- The clearest anchor available is the chronic diarrhoea definition: "persistent alteration from the norm with stool consistency between types 5 and 7 on the Bristol stool chart and increased frequency **greater than 4 weeks' duration**." `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #2, §2.1)
- "most groups including this GDG accept that symptoms persisting for longer than **4 weeks** suggest a non-infectious aetiology and merit further investigation." `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #2, §2.1)
- A change **towards constipation** has no equivalent published duration threshold in the sources read. `[UNVERIFIED]`
- **Faecal incontinence is commonly misinterpreted as diarrhoea** by patients — a "change in bowel habit" may in fact be continence, which is a different pathway. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #2, §2.1)

## 2. Who it affects

- Chronic diarrhoea prevalence 4–5% in Western populations; 7–14% in an elderly population. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #2, §2.2)
- IBS may affect 10–13% of the population, so most changes in bowel habit are **not** cancer — which is precisely why the triage rules exist. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #2, §2.3)
- **No Brazilian figure retrieved.** `[UNVERIFIED]`

## 3. Symptoms and natural history

- The differential for altered bowel habit spans: "(a) colonic neoplasia; (b) inflammation; (c) small bowel inflammation; (d) small bowel malabsorption; (e) maldigestion due to pancreatic insufficiency; or (f) motility disorders". `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #2, §2.3)
- "new onset of diarrhoea may reflect serious organic disease such as colonic neoplasia". `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #2, §1.5)

## 4. Red flags — the core of this page

**Directly citable:** `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #2, summary of recommendations)

- "We recommend **excluding colorectal cancer in those with altered bowel habit ± rectal bleeding by colonoscopy**." Evidence level 1, strength **strong**.
- "We suggest use of testing for faecal blood loss by **faecal immunochemical testing** in primary or secondary care, either as an exclusion test or to guide priority of investigations in those with lower gastrointestinal symptoms (chronic diarrhoea) but **without rectal bleeding**." Level 2, **weak**.
- "**Faecal calprotectin** is recommended to exclude colonic inflammation in those suspected with IBS and **under the age of 40**." Level 1, strong.

**The age logic, stated plainly by the guideline:** `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #2, §1.5)

- "A low threshold for the use of colonoscopy is acceptable in the context of the frequency and clinical significance of colonic neoplasia in **older patients**. However, there is less need for extensive investigation where the probability of benign disease is high (eg, in young patients with functional symptoms)."
- Audit goal: "Minimising inappropriate first-line investigations (eg, colonoscopy) in patients **less than 40 years of age without rectal bleeding and normal faecal calprotectin**."
- Audit goal: "Reduction of missed diagnoses of colorectal cancer to **<1%** through utility of faecal immunochemical testing (FIT) and endoscopic examination."

## 5. Diagnosis

First-line, from the BSG algorithm: bloods (FBC with ferritin if anaemic, CRP,
thyroid function, coeliac serology) and stool (*C. difficile*, ova/cysts/parasites,
calprotectin, consider FIT); then colonoscopy ± ileoscopy with biopsies where
colorectal cancer or IBD is suspected. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #2, Fig 1)

**Screening modalities and intervals, for the average-risk asymptomatic context
only:** `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)

| Modality | Interval |
|---|---|
| FIT or high-sensitivity gFOBT | annual |
| Stool DNA-FIT | every 1–3 years |
| CT colonography | every 5 years |
| Flexible sigmoidoscopy | every 5 years |
| Flexible sigmoidoscopy + annual FIT | every 10 years |
| Colonoscopy | every 10 years |

## 6. Screening thresholds — and why they are not the answer to a symptom

USPSTF (2021), asymptomatic average-risk adults: `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)

| Age band | Recommendation | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| 45–49 | "The USPSTF recommends screening for colorectal cancer in adults aged 45 to 49 years." | **B** |
| 50–75 | "The USPSTF recommends screening for colorectal cancer in all adults aged 50 to 75 years." | **A** |
| 76–85 | "The USPSTF recommends that clinicians **selectively** offer screening…" | **C** |

**Explicitly out of scope of that guidance:** people with a personal or family
history of known genetic disorders (Lynch syndrome, FAP), and — per the same
scope statement — general family history of colorectal cancer, and inflammatory
bowel disease. Those groups follow different pathways. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)
See the companion research file on family history of IBD.

**Brazilian context:** whether and at what age organised screening is offered in
Brazil, through SUS or ANS, was **not** checked in this run. Do not present a US
age as the Brazilian recommendation. `[UNVERIFIED]` ⚠ VERIFICAR COM A MÉDICA.

## 9. Patient questions (FAQ) — answer sketches, not copy

1. **Mudou meu intestino. A partir de quanto tempo devo procurar alguém?** — More than 4 weeks of persistent change is the threshold the guidelines use for further investigation. Source #2 §2.1.
2. **Tenho 38 anos, ainda não é idade de fazer colonoscopia, certo?** — Wrong question. Screening ages apply to people **without** symptoms. A persistent symptom is a different pathway. Sources #1, #2. **This is the page's core message.**
3. **Sangramento junto com mudança de hábito — o que muda?** — Altered bowel habit with rectal bleeding is an explicit indication to exclude colorectal cancer by colonoscopy, level 1 evidence, strong recommendation. Source #2.
4. **E se não tem sangramento?** — FIT may be used to exclude blood loss or to prioritise investigation; under 40 with no bleeding and normal calprotectin, the guideline actively aims to avoid colonoscopy. Source #2.
5. **O que é FIT / sangue oculto?** — A stool test for blood, used as an exclusion or prioritisation tool. Note it is a **weak** recommendation, level 2. Source #2.
6. **Quase sempre é câncer?** — No. IBS alone may affect 10–13% of people; most changes in bowel habit are benign. The point of the rules is to find the few without over-investigating everyone. Source #2.
7. **A partir de que idade rastrear se não tenho sintoma?** — USPSTF: 45–49 grade B, 50–75 grade A. But that is US guidance; Brazilian practice is hers to state. Source #1 + ⚠ VERIFICAR.
8. **Tenho caso de câncer de intestino na família.** — Family history takes you out of average-risk screening guidance entirely. Source #1. Specific schedule ⚠ VERIFICAR.
9. **Não é bem diarreia, é que escapa.** — Faecal incontinence is commonly mistaken for diarrhoea and is a different problem with a different pathway. Source #2 §2.1. **Coloproctology entry point.**
10. **A Dra. Dayara faz a colonoscopia?** — No, not at this location; referral and preparation guidance are part of the consultation.

## 10. Scope boundary

- Investigating a change in bowel habit is shared between **primary care, gastroenterology and coloproctology**.
- **Colonoscopy, CT colonography and capsule studies are not performed at this location.** The page describes the pathway honestly.
- Genuine coloproctology contribution: anorectal examination, distinguishing incontinence from diarrhoea, anorectal causes of altered habit, and deciding who needs escalation.
- This page must **not** read as a cancer-screening page. It is a "when does this symptom need looking at" page.

## 13. Do not state

- ❌ A **US screening age as the Brazilian recommendation** — not checked.
- ❌ That **screening ages apply to symptomatic people** — the central error to avoid.
- ❌ A **consolidated red-flag list** as guideline-derived — not retrievable; NICE blocked.
- ❌ That **narrow or pencil-thin stools** indicate cancer — folklore, unsupported by anything opened here.
- ❌ Any **cancer statistic or survival figure** — none retrieved.
- ❌ That **FIT rules out** colorectal cancer — it is a level 2, **weak** recommendation used to exclude blood loss or prioritise.
- ❌ That a **normal colonoscopy** excludes all colonic causes — microscopic colitis needs biopsies.
- ❌ That the practice performs **colonoscopy**.
- ❌ Alarmist framing. CFM: no sensationalism, no guaranteed outcome, no superiority claim.
