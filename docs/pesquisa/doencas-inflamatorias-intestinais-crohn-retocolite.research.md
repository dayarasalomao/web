## 1. Definition

- **Ulcerative colitis:** "mucosal inflammation starting distally in the rectum, with continuous extension proximally", with "an abrupt demarcation between inflamed and non-inflamed mucosa". `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)
- **Crohn's disease:** transmural inflammation that can affect any part of the gastrointestinal tract. Disease locations named: ileal, ileocolonic, colonic, jejunal, upper GI, perianal. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)
- The two distinctions that matter for a lay page: UC is **continuous and starts at the rectum**; Crohn's is **patchy, can appear anywhere from mouth to anus, and goes through the full thickness of the wall**. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)
- **Perianal** is a named Crohn's location — this is the direct link to coloproctology (section 10).

## 3. Symptoms and natural history

- UC follows a relapsing–remitting course: "up to 90% will have one or more relapses after the first attack". `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)
- Patients "experience periods of relapse and remission". `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)
- Mucosal healing matters beyond symptom control: Source #1 refers to "evidence of the benefits of mucosal healing in reducing future risk of colorectal carcinoma". `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)

## 5. Diagnosis

- **Faecal calprotectin (or lactoferrin) and CRP** are recommended in patients without alarm features with suspected IBS and diarrhoea, specifically **to rule out inflammatory bowel disease**. Strong recommendation; moderate quality for CRP and calprotectin, very low for lactoferrin. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #2, rec 2)
- **The numeric calprotectin cut-off was not retrieved.** NICE DG11, which sets it, is blocked. Do not print a µg/g threshold. `[UNVERIFIED]`
- **Endoscopy establishes the diagnosis and the extent.** Verbatim: "Where ulcerative colitis is diagnosed by sigmoidoscopy, we recommend a full ileocolonoscopy to delineate disease extent, severity of inflammation and to exclude Crohn's disease." GRADE: **strong recommendation, very low-quality evidence**. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, Statement 1)
- **Biopsy protocol:** "at least two biopsy specimens… from five sites throughout the examined bowel, including the ileum and rectum, during the initial endoscopic evaluation." `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)
- Editorial consequence: **this diagnosis cannot be made without colonoscopy with biopsies.** That is the single most important practical fact for this page, because Dra. Dayara does not perform colonoscopy at this location (section 10).

## 9. Patient questions (FAQ) — answer sketches, not copy

1. **Crohn e retocolite são a mesma doença?** — No. UC is continuous from the rectum and mucosal; Crohn's is patchy, can affect any part of the tract including the perianal region, and is transmural. Source #1.
2. **Dá para saber só pelo sintoma?** — No. Diagnosis requires ileocolonoscopy with biopsies from multiple sites. Source #1, Statement 1.
3. **Por que pediram calprotectina fecal?** — To distinguish inflammation (IBD) from a functional disorder (IBS) before deciding whether endoscopy is needed. Source #2, rec 2.
4. **É a mesma coisa que intestino irritável?** — No, and the whole point of the calprotectin/CRP step is to separate them. Source #2.
5. **Tem cura?** — Framing question; the sources describe a relapsing–remitting course, not cure. ⚠ VERIFICAR COM A MÉDICA.
6. **Vou precisar de cirurgia?** — Not retrievable from sources read. ⚠ VERIFICAR COM A MÉDICA.
7. **Tenho mais risco de câncer?** — Extensive colitis carries the highest risk, and mucosal healing reduces future risk; the surveillance schedule was not retrieved. Source #1 + ⚠ VERIFICAR.
8. **A Dra. Dayara faz a colonoscopia?** — No, not at this location. The page must say so plainly.
9. **Quem trata: gastro ou coloproctologista?** — Both, in different roles. See section 10.
10. **Fístula e abscesso perianal têm a ver com Crohn?** — Perianal is a named Crohn's disease location; this is where coloproctology is central. Source #1.

## 10. Scope boundary — important for this topic

- Medical management of IBD (immunosuppression, biologics, maintenance) is **gastroenterology**. The page must not imply the practice runs it.
- **Diagnosis requires colonoscopy with biopsies, which is not performed at this location.** The honest page describes the pathway and the referral, not the exam.
- The genuine coloproctology contribution: **perianal Crohn's disease** (abscess, fistula — territory the site already covers well), assessment of anorectal symptoms in known IBD, surgical questions, and shared surveillance. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, perianal named as a disease location)
- Suspecting IBD and routing the patient correctly is a legitimate and honest thing for the page to be about. Curing it here is not.
