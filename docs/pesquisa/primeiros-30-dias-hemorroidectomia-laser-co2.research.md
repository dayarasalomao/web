## 1. Definition
- [EXPERT-OPINION] Excision leaves a healing wound; use of CO2 as an instrument does not make excision a non-excisional technique. (Source #1; supplied procedure description)

## 2. Who it affects
- No prevalence statistic required or retained for this scope.

## 3. Symptoms and natural history
- [EXPERT-OPINION] Pain, spotting and discharge can persist beyond the first weeks. Calendar milestones cannot guarantee recovery. (Source #2)

## 4. Red flags
- [EXPERT-OPINION] Heavy bleeding requires urgent assessment; inability to void requires prompt contact/evaluation. (Sources #1, #2)
- No age-based cancer screening threshold is added; recurrent bleeding needs individualized evaluation.

## 5. Diagnosis
- [EXPERT-OPINION] History and physical assessment guide diagnosis. Images and treatment response do not replace examination. (Source #1)

## 6. Conservative vs procedural management
- [EXPERT-OPINION] Stool regulation, prescribed analgesia and gentle wound hygiene are central. Individual postoperative instructions take priority. (Source #2)

## 9. Patient questions
- Day-30 healing? Not guaranteed. Laser without cutting? Distinguish CO2 excision from diode LHP. Medication taper? Individual prescription. Bleeding? Distinguish spotting from heavy/persistent bleeding. (Sources #1, #2)

## 10. Scope boundary
- Outpatient patient education in Campo Grande/MS. Acute complications need appropriate urgent care; do not imply emergency infrastructure or in-house colonoscopy.

## 11. Terminology map
- Hemorrhoidectomy → hemorroidectomia → cirurgia com retirada de tecido.
- Hemorrhoidoplasty → hemorroidoplastia → laser de diodo, “sem corte”.
- Thrombosed external hemorrhoid → hemorroida externa trombosada → caroço duro e dolorido.
