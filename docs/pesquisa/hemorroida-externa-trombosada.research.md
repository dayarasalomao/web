## 1. Definition
- [EXPERT-OPINION] A clot in external hemorrhoidal tissue can cause a painful blue/purple lump. (Source #1)

## 2. Who it affects
- No prevalence statistic required or retained for this scope.

## 3. Symptoms and natural history
- [EXPERT-OPINION] Pain can peak in the first 48–72 hours and subsequently improve; residual skin tags can occur. Timing of pain is not an obligatory surgical deadline. (Source #1)

## 4. Red flags
- [EXPERT-OPINION] Severe/persistent pain needs assessment; fever/pus or progressive redness should not be assumed to be uncomplicated thrombosis. Differential includes perianal abscess. (Source #1; existing project abscess content)
- No age-based cancer screening threshold is added; recurrent bleeding needs individualized evaluation.

## 5. Diagnosis
- [EXPERT-OPINION] History and physical assessment guide diagnosis. Images and treatment response do not replace examination. (Source #1)

## 6. Conservative vs procedural management
- [EXPERT-OPINION] Improving symptoms may support conservative care; severe pain can justify an office procedure following clinical assessment. (Source #1)
