## 1. Definition

- **Verbatim:** "chronic diarrhoea is the persistent alteration from the norm with stool consistency between types 5 and 7 on the Bristol stool chart and increased frequency greater than 4 weeks' duration." `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, §2.1)
- Bristol **type 5 and above** is the recommended consistency threshold. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, §2.1)
- **Stool weight (≥200 g/day) is explicitly NOT recommended** as a measure of diarrhoea — stool weights vary greatly and normal volumes can exceed that value, particularly on non-Western diets. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, §2.1)
- "There is no consensus on the duration of symptoms that define chronic as opposed to acute diarrhoea. However, most groups including this GDG accept that symptoms persisting for longer than 4 weeks suggest a non-infectious aetiology and merit further investigation." `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, §2.1)
- **Editorially the most useful line in the whole guideline:** "Faecal incontinence in particular is commonly misinterpreted as diarrhoea" by patients, and the medical vs lay concepts of diarrhoea diverge. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, §2.1). This is squarely coloproctology and is a genuine reason for this page to exist.

## 2. Who it affects

- Prevalence in a **Western population: 4–5%**, using a definition based on excessive stool frequency without abdominal pain. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, §2.2)
- **7%–14% in an elderly population** across two symptom surveys (Talley et al), a proportion of which is likely to be motility disorders, i.e. functional bowel disease. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, §2.2)
- IBS may affect **10%–13%** of the population, which is why diarrhoea reporting overlaps so heavily with functional disease. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, §2.3)
- "Chronic diarrhoea is one of the most common reasons for referral to a gastroenterology clinic." `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, §2.2)
- **No Brazilian figure retrieved.** `[UNVERIFIED]`

## 3. Symptoms and natural history

- Diarrhoea may result from: "(a) colonic neoplasia; (b) inflammation; (c) small bowel inflammation; (d) small bowel malabsorption; (e) maldigestion due to pancreatic insufficiency; or (f) motility disorders". `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, §2.3)
- "new onset of diarrhoea may reflect serious organic disease such as colonic neoplasia" — the reason a low threshold for colonoscopy is accepted in older patients. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, §1.5)

## 4. Red flags

The guideline does not print a single boxed red-flag list; the alarm logic sits
inside the recommendations. What is citable:

- **Altered bowel habit ± rectal bleeding → exclude colorectal cancer by colonoscopy.** Evidence level 1, strength **strong**. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, summary)
- **Age is the pivot.** Audit goal 3: "Minimising inappropriate first-line investigations (eg, colonoscopy) in patients **less than 40 years of age without rectal bleeding and normal faecal calprotectin**." `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, §1.5)
- "A low threshold for the use of colonoscopy is acceptable in the context of the frequency and clinical significance of colonic neoplasia in **older patients**. However, there is less need for extensive investigation where the probability of benign disease is high (eg, in young patients with functional symptoms)." `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, §1.5)
- Malabsorption and infection screening is emphasised **especially in the immunocompromised or elderly**. Level 2, strong. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, summary)

## 5. Diagnosis — the investigation algorithm

**First line (primary clinical assessment), from Figure 1:** `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, Fig 1)

- **Blood:** FBC (ferritin if anaemic), CRP, thyroid function, coeliac serology
- **Stool:** *C. difficile*; ova, cysts and parasites; calprotectin; consider FIT

With the graded recommendations behind them:

- "We recommend a careful detailed history to plan investigations." Level 1, strong.
- "We recommend screening blood tests for the exclusion of anaemia, coeliac disease, etc as well as stool tests for inflammation." Level 1, strong.
- "We recommend making a **positive diagnosis of irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)** following basic blood and stool screening tests." Level 2, strong. — consistent with the IBS run: positive diagnosis, not exclusion.
- "**Faecal calprotectin is recommended to exclude colonic inflammation in those suspected with IBS and under the age of 40.**" Level 1, strong.
- FIT "either as an exclusion test or to guide priority of investigations in those with lower gastrointestinal symptoms (chronic diarrhoea) but without rectal bleeding." Level 2, **weak**.

**Then, by suspicion:** `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, Fig 1)

| Suspected | Investigation |
|---|---|
| Colorectal cancer or IBD | Colonoscopy ± ileoscopy and biopsies (consider flexible sigmoidoscopy in younger patients) |
| Coeliac disease | Upper GI endoscopy and biopsy |
| Small bowel IBD | MR enterography or capsule endoscopy (CT if neither possible) |
| Bile acid diarrhoea | SeHCAT; C4 blood test or faecal bile acid measurement if unavailable |
| Microscopic colitis | Lower GI endoscopy **with biopsy** |
| Lactose malabsorption | Hydrogen breath test, 25 g lactose challenge |

- **Microscopic colitis, verbatim:** "We recommend colonoscopy with **biopsies of right and left colon (not rectal)** to exclude microscopic colitis." Level 1, strong. — the mucosa looks normal; only histology makes this diagnosis.
- **Bile acid diarrhoea:** "In those with functional bowel or IBS-diarrhoea, a positive diagnosis of bile acid diarrhoea should be made either by SeHCAT testing or serum bile acid precursor 7α-hydroxy-4-cholesten-3-one (depending on local availability)." Level 1, strong. And: "There is insufficient evidence to recommend use of an empirical trial of treatment for bile acid diarrhoea rather than making a positive diagnosis." Level 5, strong.

**Tests the guideline recommends AGAINST:** `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, summary)

- **Small bowel barium follow through / barium enteroclysis** — not recommended, poor sensitivity and specificity. Level 5, strong.
- **PABA testing** — not recommended (faecal elastase instead when fat malabsorption suspected). Level 1, strong.
- **Routine hydrogen or methane breath testing for SIBO** — insufficient evidence; empirical antibiotic trial recommended instead. Level 3, strong.
- **Enteroscopy** only for targeted lesions identified by MR enterography or capsule endoscopy, "and not for diagnosis of chronic diarrhoea". Level 4, strong.
- **Hormone-secreting tumours:** rare, so test "only when other causes of diarrhoea have been excluded". Level 4, strong.

## 7. What the procedure involves

The coloproctology-relevant procedural line, verbatim: "We recommend use of
**anorectal manometry and endoanal ultrasound only when other local pathology
has been excluded and conservative measures exhausted**." Level 3, strong.
`[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, summary). Fistulae: MRI or CT with contrast follow
through. Level 3, strong.

## 8. Outcomes

⚠ VERIFICAR COM A MÉDICA — no treatment outcome rates, by design (§6). The
guideline's own audit target is diagnostic rather than therapeutic: "Reduction of
missed diagnoses of colorectal cancer to **<1%** through utility of faecal
immunochemical testing (FIT) and endoscopic examination." `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, §1.5)

## 9. Patient questions (FAQ) — answer sketches, not copy

1. **A partir de quando a diarreia é "crônica"?** — More than 4 weeks, with Bristol type 5–7 consistency and increased frequency. Source #1 §2.1.
2. **Preciso pesar as fezes / contar quantas vezes?** — Stool weight is explicitly not recommended as a measure; consistency and frequency are what count. Source #1 §2.1.
3. **Pode ser só "intestino solto" e não doença?** — Yes: functional bowel disease and IBS-D are positive diagnoses made after basic blood and stool screening, not leftovers. Source #1 summary.
4. **Que exames vou fazer primeiro?** — Bloods (FBC, ferritin if anaemic, CRP, thyroid, coeliac serology) and stool (C. difficile, parasites, calprotectin, possibly FIT). Source #1 Fig 1.
5. **Vou precisar de colonoscopia?** — Depends on age, rectal bleeding and calprotectin. Under 40, without bleeding and with normal calprotectin, the guideline actively aims to avoid it. Source #1 §1.5.
6. **O que é calprotectina fecal?** — A stool marker of bowel inflammation, recommended to exclude colonic inflammation in suspected IBS under 40. Source #1 summary.
7. **Colonoscopia normal e ainda tenho diarreia. E agora?** — Microscopic colitis looks normal to the eye; it needs right and left colon biopsies. Also consider bile acid diarrhoea. Source #1 summary.
8. **O que é diarreia por ácidos biliares?** — A specific, testable cause common in functional bowel/IBS-D, diagnosed by SeHCAT or serum C4 rather than by trial of treatment. Source #1 summary.
9. **Posso ter intolerância à lactose?** — Tested by hydrogen breath test with a 25 g lactose challenge, or by dietary withdrawal. Level 3, weak — a softer recommendation than the others. Source #1 summary.
10. **Não é diarreia, acho que estou perdendo fezes sem querer.** — Faecal incontinence is commonly misinterpreted as diarrhoea, and it is a different problem with a different pathway. Source #1 §2.1. **This is the coloproctology entry point.**

## Absence of evidence — report, per the brief

- **Verbatim:** "Specific RCTs addressing the investigation of 'chronic diarrhoea' are absent." Grading was therefore based on Oxford CEBM evidence levels 1–5. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, §1.3)
- Several recommendations are strong on **level 5** evidence (expert opinion) — e.g. referral after normal first-line tests, and not using an empirical BAD trial. Worth knowing before the page states them with confidence.

## Guidelines differ — age threshold

| Source | Threshold |
|---|---|
| BSG chronic diarrhoea (Source #1) | calprotectin to exclude inflammation in suspected IBS **under 40**; avoid colonoscopy under 40 without bleeding and with normal calprotectin |
| ACG IBS (Source #2) | against routine colonoscopy in IBS symptoms **under 45** without warning signs |

Different populations and different questions, but the page should not print a
single age as "the" cut-off. Say the threshold depends on the pathway and the
individual.

## 10. Scope boundary

- Investigation of chronic diarrhoea is largely **gastroenterology / primary care**, and the guideline is addressed to "consultant gastroenterologists, gastrointestinal surgeons, specialist registrars in training and general practitioners". `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, §1.1)
- **Colonoscopy, upper GI endoscopy, capsule endoscopy, MR enterography and SeHCAT are not performed at this location.** The page describes the pathway and the referral.
- Genuine coloproctology contribution: distinguishing **faecal incontinence** from diarrhoea, anorectal manometry and endoanal ultrasound where indicated, and fistula assessment. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1, summary)

## 13. Do not state

- ❌ A **Brazilian prevalence** figure — none retrieved.
- ❌ **Stool weight** (200 g/day) as the definition of diarrhoea — explicitly not recommended.
- ❌ A single **age cut-off** as "the" threshold for colonoscopy — the two guidelines read here differ (40 vs 45) for different questions.
- ❌ Any **treatment** claim sourced to this guideline — therapy is explicitly outside its remit.
- ❌ That a **normal colonoscopy rules out** a colonic cause — microscopic colitis requires right and left colon biopsies.
- ❌ That **breath testing** reliably diagnoses SIBO — insufficient evidence per this guideline.
- ❌ That the practice performs **colonoscopy, SeHCAT, capsule endoscopy or MR enterography**.
- ❌ Any suggestion that **diarrhoea equals incontinence** or vice versa — the distinction is the point.
- ❌ CFM: no guaranteed outcome, no superiority claim, no alarmism about cancer.
