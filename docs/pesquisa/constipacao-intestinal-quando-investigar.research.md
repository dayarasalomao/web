## 1. Definition

- Diagnosis requires **any 2 of 6** criteria: fewer than 3 bowel movements weekly; straining; hard or lumpy stools; sensation of incomplete evacuation; sensation of blockage; use of manual manoeuvres. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)
- "Functional constipation", "chronic constipation" and "CIC" refer to the **same patient group**. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)
- The full Rome IV wording, including the duration criteria, was **not** in the source read. `[UNVERIFIED]`
- **The distinction that matters most for a coloproctology page:** chronic idiopathic constipation is not the same as a **defecatory disorder**. Verbatim: "A patient could have a defecation disorder (eg, dyssynergic defecation) that may not respond to laxatives." `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)

## 3. Symptoms and natural history

- The six diagnostic criteria above are also the symptom list patients recognise: infrequency, straining, hard stool, incomplete evacuation, blockage, manual manoeuvres. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)
- **"Manual manoeuvres" is worth its own sentence on the page.** Patients rarely volunteer it and it points towards a defecatory disorder rather than slow transit — i.e. towards anorectal assessment rather than more laxatives.

## 4. Red flags

- The guideline's algorithm begins here. Verbatim: "The first step is to look for **alarm features** to rule out a structural disease, like colon cancer, for example." `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)
- **The list itself was not in the source read.** `[UNVERIFIED]` ⚠ VERIFICAR COM A MÉDICA — same gap as the other topics; ask Dra. Dayara for her list.
- Compare with the chronic diarrhoea guideline, which is explicit that altered bowel habit ± rectal bleeding warrants colonoscopy to exclude colorectal cancer, level 1 evidence, strong. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #2, summary) — the same logic applies to a change in bowel habit toward constipation.

## 5. Diagnosis

- Two things must be excluded before treating as idiopathic: **structural disease** (alarm features → investigate) and **secondary causes**. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)
- **Anorectal manometry** is the key test, and its indication is specific: "If a dyssynergic defecation is suspected, clinicians should refer the patient for anorectal manometry to make sure this diagnosis is not missed." Indicated when patients fail to respond adequately to standard pharmacological therapy. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)
- Dyssynergic defecation is treated with **biofeedback therapy, not oral medication**. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)
- Cross-reference from the diarrhoea guideline, same modality, more restrictive framing: "We recommend use of anorectal manometry and endoanal ultrasound **only when other local pathology has been excluded and conservative measures exhausted**." Level 3, strong. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #2)

## 6. Conservative vs pharmacological management

**Non-pharmacological first:** dietary measures (increased fluid, increased dietary fibre) and behavioural change (exercise) "often represent the initial steps in management". `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)

**Fibre — which fibre matters:** `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)

- Fibre supplementation is suggested over management without it. **Conditional**, low/very low certainty.
- Verbatim: "Among the evaluated fiber supplements, **only psyllium appears to be effective**, with very limited and uncertain data on bran and inulin."
- Can be used first-line "particularly for individuals with low-fiber intake"; dietary assessment of total fibre intake and adequate hydration are emphasised.
- **Consistent with the IBS finding** (Source #3): soluble fibre such as ispaghula/psyllium helps; insoluble fibre such as wheat bran should be avoided. Two independent guidelines pointing the same way is worth saying on the page.

**Pharmacological, from the 2023 AGA–ACG guideline:** `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)

- **Verbatim and important:** "No treatment had a high certainty of evidence." `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)
- This guideline is reported as **the first to recommend magnesium oxide and senna** as evidence-based treatments. `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1, characterising the guideline) ⚠ VERIFICAR COM A MÉDICA — interesting but it is a framing claim from a society press summary, not a graded recommendation.

> **Note the conflict with the IBS guidelines.** ACG's IBS guideline suggests
> *against* PEG for global IBS-C symptoms and BSG says it may help the
> constipation itself (see the IBS research file). Here, for chronic idiopathic
> constipation as its own diagnosis, PEG is a **strong** recommendation on
> moderate certainty. The distinction is the diagnosis, not the drug — a useful
> thing for the page to make clear rather than blur.

## 9. Patient questions (FAQ) — answer sketches, not copy

1. **Quantas vezes por semana é "normal"?** — Fewer than 3 bowel movements weekly is one of six criteria, and only two of the six are needed; frequency alone does not define it. Source #1.
2. **Preciso evacuar todo dia?** — Not supported by the criteria. Source #1. ⚠ VERIFICAR COM A MÉDICA for the phrasing she prefers.
3. **Quando investigar em vez de só tratar?** — When alarm features are present, structural disease must be excluded first. Source #1.
4. **Tomo laxante e não resolve. Por quê?** — Possibly a defecatory disorder such as dyssynergic defecation, which "may not respond to laxatives" and needs anorectal manometry rather than a stronger laxative. Source #1. **This is the single most useful item on the page.**
5. **Preciso usar o dedo para conseguir evacuar. Isso é normal?** — Manual manoeuvres are one of the diagnostic criteria and point towards a defecatory disorder. Source #1.
6. **Fibra resolve? Qual?** — Only psyllium has supporting evidence among supplements; bran and inulin data are limited and uncertain. Source #1; consistent with Source #3.
7. **Laxante faz mal / vicia?** — Not answerable from the sources read. ⚠ VERIFICAR COM A MÉDICA — common patient fear, needs her position.
8. **O que é biofeedback?** — The treatment for dyssynergic defecation, in place of oral medication. Source #1.
9. **Preciso de colonoscopia?** — Only if alarm features suggest structural disease; the list is hers to give. Source #1 + ⚠ VERIFICAR.
10. **Constipação é o mesmo que síndrome do intestino irritável com constipação?** — Different diagnoses with different drug recommendations (see the PEG note above). Sources #1, #3.

## 10. Scope boundary

- Pharmacological management of chronic constipation is shared with **gastroenterology and primary care**.
- The genuine coloproctology contribution is the **defecatory disorder** half: recognising dyssynergic defecation, anorectal manometry, pelvic floor assessment and biofeedback. This is also the part patients most often reach after laxatives have failed. `[GUIDELINE]` (Sources #1, #2)
- **Colonoscopy is not performed at this location.** Where alarm features indicate it, the page describes referral.
- Several drugs named above are prescription agents whose **Brazilian availability was not checked**. `[UNVERIFIED]` — do not list drug names on the page without confirming.

## 13. Do not state

- ❌ Any **prevalence** figure — none retrieved.
- ❌ That someone must evacuate **daily** — not supported by the criteria.
- ❌ Any **alarm-feature list** presented as guideline-derived — not retrieved.
- ❌ That **any treatment is well proven** — verbatim, "no treatment had a high certainty of evidence".
- ❌ That **bran or inulin** are effective supplements — data limited and uncertain; only psyllium has support.
- ❌ Naming **prescription drugs** without checking Brazilian availability.
- ❌ That **laxatives are the answer when they have already failed** — that is the defecatory-disorder signal.
- ❌ That the practice performs **colonoscopy**.
- ❌ Blurring **CIC** and **IBS-C** — the same drug (PEG) carries opposite recommendations across the two diagnoses.
- ❌ CFM: no guaranteed outcome, no superiority claim, no alarmism.
