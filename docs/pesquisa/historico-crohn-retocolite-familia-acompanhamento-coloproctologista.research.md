## 1. The one thing that is solid

**People with a family history of colorectal cancer, and people with
inflammatory bowel disease, are explicitly OUTSIDE average-risk screening
guidance.** `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)

USPSTF's colorectal cancer screening recommendation applies to "asymptomatic
adults 45 years or older who are at **average risk**", and explicitly excludes
those with a personal or family history of known genetic disorders such as Lynch
syndrome or familial adenomatous polyposis; general family history of colorectal
cancer and inflammatory bowel disease fall outside that average-risk guidance
as well. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #1)

**Editorially this is the whole page in one sentence:** the schedule you read
about for everyone else is not your schedule, and the guideline says so itself.
What your schedule *is* has to be set individually — which is the honest reason
to book an appointment rather than a marketing line.

## 5. Diagnosis — for the relative who is worried

What *is* citable, from the companion research:

- IBD is diagnosed by **ileocolonoscopy with biopsies**, not by symptoms or blood tests alone. Verbatim: "Where ulcerative colitis is diagnosed by sigmoidoscopy, we recommend a full ileocolonoscopy to delineate disease extent, severity of inflammation and to exclude Crohn's disease." Strong recommendation, very low-quality evidence. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #2)
- Biopsy protocol: "at least two biopsy specimens… from five sites throughout the examined bowel, including the ileum and rectum". `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #2)
- **Faecal calprotectin and CRP** are the non-invasive step used to decide whether inflammation is present at all, recommended in suspected IBS with diarrhoea and no alarm features specifically to rule out IBD. Strong; moderate. `[GUIDELINE]` (Source #3, rec 2)
- So a worried relative **with symptoms** has a defined first step that is not a colonoscopy: bloods and stool markers. A worried relative **without symptoms** has no retrieved basis for routine testing at all. `[UNVERIFIED]` ⚠ VERIFICAR — this is a question the page will definitely be asked and must not answer by guessing.

## 10. Scope boundary

- Surveillance in IBD is typically **gastroenterology-led**, with endoscopy performed elsewhere.
- **Colonoscopy is not performed at this location**, so a page about surveillance must be explicit that the exam happens elsewhere and that the practice's role is assessment, anorectal problems, perianal Crohn's disease and coordination.
- Perianal Crohn's disease is the part that is genuinely and centrally coloproctology, and the site already covers fistula and abscess well — link there.
- **Risk of overreach on this page is high.** A page about family risk and surveillance can easily read as "book a check-up here" for something the practice does not perform. It must not.
