## 1. Definition

- HS is a "chronic inflammatory, recurrent, and debilitating skin disorder" affecting apocrine-gland-bearing regions: axillae, inframammary folds, groin, perineal and perigenital areas. `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1)
- **Three diagnostic criteria, all required:** `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1)
  1. **Typical lesions** — "painful nodules, abscesses, suppurative sinus tracts, coalescing scars, and complex comedones"
  2. **Typical sites** — at least one of axillary, inframammary, inguinal, perineal, perianal or gluteal
  3. **Chronicity** — "persisting lesions over 3 months or two recurrences within 6 months"
- Also called **acne inversa**. `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1)

## 2. Who it affects

- Prevalence **1–4%** in young European populations; **0.13%** in a Minnesota (USA) population. `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1) — note the two-order-of-magnitude gap between these figures; do not present either as "the" prevalence.
- **Three females to one male** overall, but **perineal disease is more prevalent in males**. `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1)
- **Diagnostic delay reaches 12 years** before correct diagnosis. `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1)
- **No Brazilian figure retrieved.** `[UNVERIFIED]`

> The brief asks explicitly to note documented delay in seeking or receiving
> care. **Twelve years is the single most important number on this page** and is
> the honest reason for the page to exist: people carry this for a decade being
> treated for recurrent "boils".

## 3. Symptoms and natural history

- Painful nodules and abscesses that recur in the same regions, progressing to sinus tracts ("tunnels"), scarring and complex comedones. `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1)
- Course is chronic and recurrent by definition. `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1)

## 4. Red flags / severity staging

**Hurley staging, verbatim:** `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1)

| Stage | Definition |
|---|---|
| **I — mild** | "Single or multiple abscesses without sinus tracts and scarring" |
| **II — moderate** | "Recurrent abscesses with sinus tracts and scarring" |
| **III — severe** | "Diffuse or multiple interconnected sinus tracts and abscesses across the entire area" |

- The **IHS4** score is the other severity instrument in current use, and the European S2k guideline classifies an inflammatory form by IHS4 alongside Hurley for the predominantly non-inflammatory form. **The IHS4 scoring detail and its severity bands were not retrieved** — the guideline carrying them is blocked. `[UNVERIFIED]`
- Comorbidity screening: metabolic syndrome affects **≥50%** of HS patients; screening for metabolic and inflammatory comorbidities is a guideline recommendation. `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1) / `[UNVERIFIED]` for the guideline wording.
- ⚠ VERIFICAR COM A MÉDICA — what she wants a patient to act on: which presentation should bring someone in now.

## 5. Diagnosis — the differential is the whole point for this site

This is the section that justifies the page being on a coloproctology site rather
than a dermatology one. `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1)

| Confused with | What distinguishes it |
|---|---|
| **Pilonidal disease** | "Absence of midline pits over the sacrum" differentiates HS from pilonidal disease |
| **Crohn's disease (perianal)** | "Absence of involvement of the anal canal distinguishes HS from CD". Roughly **50% of Crohn's patients develop perianal lesions very similar to HS** |
| **Benign anal fistula** | Same discriminator — no anal canal involvement. HS tunnels connect glands to the skin surface rather than involving the anal canal or rectum |

Other named differentials: scrofuloderma, granuloma inguinale, simple abscesses.

- **The operative question is whether the anal canal is involved.** That is an anorectal examination, and it is what a coloproctologist is for.
- The site already has strong pages on **pilonidal disease**, **anal fistula** and **perianal abscess**. This page's job is to be the fourth corner of that differential and link to the other three.

## 6. Treatment by severity

**Stage I–II:** `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1)

- Topical: clindamycin lotion 1%; intralesional corticosteroids
- Systemic: oral tetracycline; retinoids; antiandrogen therapy
- Surgical: less invasive approaches — deroofing, curettage, laser

**Stage II–III:** `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1)

- Systemic: combination rifampicin + clindamycin for 10 weeks; biologics — adalimumab reported with "58.9% achieved at least 50% reduction in acute lesion count" ⚠ VERIFICAR COM A MÉDICA (single reported figure from a narrative chapter; do not publish without checking it against the trial)
- Surgical: wide radical excision; "best outcomes achieved with large resections and reconstructive procedures"

**Adjuvant / supportive across stages:** `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1)

- **Smoking cessation** — "one of the pillars of treatment is discouraging smoking"
- Weight loss
- Pain control and psychosocial support
- Hyperbaric oxygen reported with "shorter healing time" post-operatively ⚠ VERIFICAR — not standard of care; treat as a claim, not a service.

**Modifiable risk factors:** `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1)

- **Smoking:** associated with **60–70%** of patients with perianal HS
- **Obesity:** HS "10 times more prevalent in the morbidly obese compared to general population"; obesity aggravates HS by retaining sweat and breaking follicular and glandular ostia
- Genetic predisposition in up to **40%**

> Careful with tone here. Smoking and weight are causal factors in the sources,
> and they are also exactly where a patient-education page can slip into blame.
> The CFM framing matters: state the association, state that treatment does not
> depend on the patient fixing it first.

## 7. What the procedure involves

Deroofing, curettage and laser for limited disease; wide excision with
reconstruction for extensive disease. The chapter states outcomes are best with
large resections and reconstruction, but gives no recurrence rates.
`[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1)

- The practice has **CO₂ laser** available (brief practice context). Whether laser is used for HS specifically in this practice is **for Dra. Dayara to say** — do not infer it from equipment. ⚠ VERIFICAR COM A MÉDICA.

## 9. Patient questions (FAQ) — answer sketches, not copy

1. **Isso é só um furúnculo que volta?** — No. Recurrent painful nodules and abscesses in the same regions, over 3 months or twice in 6 months, meet a diagnostic definition with a name. Source #1.
2. **Por que demorou tanto para alguém dar um nome?** — Diagnostic delay reaches 12 years. It is a documented feature of this disease, not a personal failure. Source #1.
3. **É contagioso? É falta de higiene?** — Not supported by anything in the sources; the mechanism described is follicular/glandular, with genetic predisposition up to 40%. Source #1. ⚠ VERIFICAR — she may want to answer this one directly, it is the question patients actually carry.
4. **Tem a ver com doença de Crohn?** — They can look almost identical in the perianal region; about 50% of Crohn's patients develop similar perianal lesions. Anal canal involvement is the discriminator. Source #1.
5. **Como diferencia de cisto pilonidal?** — Absence of midline pits over the sacrum. Source #1.
6. **Como diferencia de fístula anal?** — HS tunnels run between glands and skin without involving the anal canal. Source #1.
7. **Fumar piora?** — Smoking is associated with 60–70% of perianal HS, and discouraging smoking is described as a pillar of treatment. Source #1.
8. **Preciso emagrecer antes de tratar?** — Obesity aggravates it, but framing matters. ⚠ VERIFICAR COM A MÉDICA.
9. **Vou precisar de cirurgia?** — Depends on Hurley stage; limited disease has less invasive options, extensive disease is excision with reconstruction. Source #1.
10. **Tem cura?** — No cure claim is supportable from the source read. ⚠ VERIFICAR COM A MÉDICA.

## 10. Scope boundary

- HS is fundamentally a **dermatological** disease; the European guideline is a dermatology guideline and biologics (adalimumab, secukinumab, bimekizumab) are dermatology/rheumatology prescribing.
- The **coloproctology contribution is real and specific**: perianal and perineal disease, the differential against Crohn's, pilonidal disease and anal fistula, examination of the anal canal to make that distinction, drainage and surgical management of perianal lesions.
- The chapter emphasises that perineal lesion management "should involve a multidisciplinary approach". The page should say so — shared care, not sole care. `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source #1)
- Do not imply the practice prescribes or manages **biologics**.
